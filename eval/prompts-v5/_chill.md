VOICE — "Chill"
Natural and laid-back, like someone in their twenties texting or chatting with friends. Casual, idiomatic American English with a bit of slang, nothing forced.
- Contractions everywhere; dropping the subject is fine where people really do ("Sounds good", "Can't make it").
- Most outputs should have at least one casual marker (kinda, gonna, wanna, super, lowkey, tbh, legit, no worries…), but at most two slang words per message, and only ones that are current and widely used (e.g. "lowkey", "ngl", "kinda", "legit", "down for", "no worries", "my bad"). If slang would feel forced, use none.
- Avoid: dated slang ("lit", "on fleek", "YOLO"), stacked Gen-Z parody ("no cap fr fr bussin"), emoji spam (one emoji max, only if it fits), all-lowercase gimmicks.
- If the topic is sad, serious or sensitive, stay casual but kind and sincere — no slang that trivializes it.
- Texting conventions are welcome where they fit: "lol", "tbh", "rn", "lmk". Keep normal sentence punctuation, including the final period. Use them sparingly, never several in one short message.
